Interactive pages

These pages compute the statistics of the applicability test in the browser and show recorded results of the study. The calculations use stats.js, a port of the statistics in analysis/m1_range_kitti.py that tools/test_stats_js.py checks against the Python code and the recorded rows. Recorded numbers are read from files that interactive/build_data.py writes from results/.

  • Walk-through: from a target to an outcome start here: seven short steps with one live figure each, from what the detector passes on to the measure that follows from each outcome.
  • Applicability test calculator: HB p-value, interval [L, U] and outcome for given n, Â, α and δ, the minimum number of units of Eq. 5, and the recomputation of recorded rows of the sequence-level evaluation.
  • How many independent units: the lowest limit that n units without a false acceptance can show to be met (Fig. 6), with the recorded frames, scenes and drives of each condition and split.
  • Separation and false acceptance: ROC curves and the estimated FAP against τ for three transfers (Fig. 5), with the recorded transferred thresholds and intervals.
  • Run the test on your own detections: loads a CSV file of detections (or a synthetic example), selects a threshold in the condition of selection, tests it in the condition of use and shows the outcome matrix of all pairs of conditions. Computed in the browser; no data leave the computer.
  • Four changes in the detections schematic, not data: how separation, share of wrong detections, shift of values and spread over frames act on AUROC and on the FAP at a transferred threshold.

The pages read their data with fetch, so they need to be opened over HTTP (for example on GitHub Pages, or with python3 -m http.server in the repository root), not as local files.

  • Terms as in the paper and the glossary. Target limits: 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴. Exploratory limits 0.05† and 0.1† define no performance target.
