Four changes in the detections schematic, not data

Sect. 3.4 of the paper names four changes in the detections from the condition of selection to the condition of use that can make a transferred threshold exceed its limit: weaker separation, more wrong detections, shifted values and a different spread over frames. This page illustrates them with invented distributions. Nothing on this page is measured; all values come from the schematic model below and are not results of the study.

Model: the indicator value of a correct detection is normally distributed with standard deviation 0.1 around 0.3 + shift, that of a wrong detection around the same centre plus separation × 0.1. A detection is accepted if its value does not exceed the fixed transferred threshold τ = 0.4. AUROC and the pooled share are computed from the two distributions. The FAP is the mean over frames of the per-frame share of false acceptances among the accepted detections, computed on a fixed simulated set of 500 frames with 1 to 8 detections each (seeded random numbers, the same for both conditions, so equal settings give equal results). The spread over frames concentrates the wrong detections in frames with few detections while keeping their expected share.

What to try: change only the separation, and AUROC and the FAP both change. Change the share of wrong detections, the shift or the concentration, and AUROC stays the same while the FAP changes. The pooled share does not depend on the spread over frames; the FAP, a mean over frames, does. In the paper, the outcome in rain was accompanied by more wrong detections and a different spread over frames, and the outcome between the datasets by shifted values (Sect. 5.2).
