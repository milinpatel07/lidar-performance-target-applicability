Separation and false acceptance

Interactive version of Fig. 5 of the paper, on the test part of split 0. Left: the ROC curve of the condition of selection (solid) and of the condition of use (dashed), with wrong detections rejected against correct detections withheld. Right: the estimated false acceptance proportion (FAP) against the threshold τ. The area under the ROC curve (AUROC) measures only how well the indicator separates correct from wrong detections. It does not depend on the share of wrong detections, on how they are spread over frames, or on where the values lie on the scale of the indicator. The FAP at a transferred threshold depends on all of these.

The slider moves the markers along both FAP curves (nearest recorded curve point). The recorded ROC points carry no threshold value, so the ROC panel marks only the operating points recorded for the two transferred thresholds; the buttons set τ to one of them and highlight its operating points.

  • Data: data/separation.json, which interactive/build_data.py writes from results/supporting_analyses/fig_separation_curves.csv. The curves are thinned as recorded (at most about 400 points per curve). AUROC is computed here from the recorded ROC points by the trapezoidal rule, as in tools/figures/paper/make_fig_separation.py.
  • The interval [L, U] is recorded only for the threshold selected at the exploratory limit 0.1†, which defines no performance target. Frames are the units here (frame-level evaluation); frames of one recorded sequence are not independent, so the stated confidence is not guaranteed.
  • In the change of dataset and detector, the threshold selected with the nuScenes detector on nuScenes is used with the KITTI detector on KITTI (KITTI rule).
